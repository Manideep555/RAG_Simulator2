Step 2 of 8 · Chunking
Cutting documents into bite-sized pieces
💡

Imagine trying to search through a huge book — it's slow and messy. Instead, the AI cuts each document into smaller chunks, like tearing pages into index cards. Each card covers one topic, making it easy to find the right one later.

Overlap means each card shares a little text with the next one, so no information falls through the cracks.

Step 3 of 8 · Embeddings
Giving each chunk a unique "fingerprint"
💡

Here's the magic step! The AI reads each chunk and converts it into a list of numbers called an embedding — think of it like a unique colour fingerprint for that text.

Chunks with similar meaning get similar fingerprints. So "dog" and "puppy" would have very similar number patterns, while "dog" and "bicycle" would look very different. This is how the AI understands meaning, not just keywords!

Step 4 of 8 · Vector Store
Filing everything into the AI's library
💡

All the chunks and their fingerprints are now saved into a special database called a vector store. Think of it as a perfectly organised filing cabinet where every folder is labelled with its fingerprint.

When a question comes in, the AI can scan this cabinet instantly — even if there are millions of documents!

Step 5 of 8 · Your Question
Ask anything about your documents
💡

Now it's your turn! Type any question you'd like the AI to answer. Your question will go through the exact same fingerprinting process as the chunks — so it can be compared to them fairly.

The AI doesn't search for exact words. It searches for similar meaning. So asking "How does AI avoid making things up?" will find chunks about "reducing hallucinations" even without using those exact words!

Step 6 of 8 · Semantic Retrieval
Finding the most relevant chunks
💡

The AI compares your question's fingerprint to every chunk's fingerprint using cosine similarity — a fancy name for measuring "how alike are these two patterns?"

It scores every chunk from 0% (totally unrelated) to 100% (perfect match), then picks the top 3 most relevant chunks to use as context. The higher the bar, the better the match!

📊 Relevance Scores — Your Question vs. Every Chunk
📐 Why "cosine" similarity? Imagine each fingerprint as an arrow pointing in a direction in space. Cosine similarity measures how closely two arrows point the same way — if they're parallel, they're a perfect match!
📋
Step 7 of 8 · Prompt Augmentation
Writing the AI's cheat sheet
💡

Before the AI writes its answer, it gets a carefully prepared briefing document — like a cheat sheet written just for your question. This briefing contains three parts:
① Instructions — how to behave and cite sources
② Context — the top matching chunks from your documents
③ Your Question — clearly stated at the end

This is the "augmented" prompt — your question augmented with real facts!

📄 The AI's Briefing Document
🎯 This is the key insight of RAG: Instead of asking the AI to "remember" facts from training, we hand it the facts right before it answers. That's why it's called Retrieval-Augmented Generation.
✨

The AI writes its answer!
💡

Finally! The AI reads the briefing document and writes a clear answer — using only the facts from your documents. It cites where each piece of information came from, just like a good essay. No guessing, no making things up!

📖 Plain-English Glossary
The jargon, explained in human language
🔢
Embedding
A list of numbers that represents the meaning of a piece of text. Similar meanings produce similar numbers.
Like a fingerprint — unique, but similar things look similar.
📐
Cosine Similarity
A way to measure how "alike" two embeddings are, on a scale from 0 (nothing in common) to 1 (identical meaning).
Like checking if two arrows point the same direction.
✂️
Chunking
Breaking long documents into smaller, focused pieces so the AI can find and use just the relevant part.
Like cutting a book into index cards, one topic each.
🗄️
Vector Store
A special database that stores text alongside its numerical fingerprint, enabling super-fast meaning-based search.
A perfectly organised filing cabinet you can search by idea, not just keyword.
🛡️
Hallucination
When an AI confidently makes up information that isn't true, because it's guessing from training data rather than real sources.
Like a student bluffing an exam answer instead of looking it up.
🧠
RAG
Retrieval-Augmented Generation. An AI technique that looks things up before answering, dramatically reducing errors.
Like giving the AI an open-book exam instead of testing its memory.
